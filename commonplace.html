The Symposium

Plato

Finished Sep 27, 2026

Just two weeks after finishing ‘The Republic’, I wandered a little to find a book that should be my next read. I tried a couple of books and even brought them along with me to Cebu City, but I couldn’t stick to any of them at that time. Just a day before I got my salary, I returned to the same bookstore in Ayala mall and blindly bought this book. Luckily, it was not that bad a decision. I had quite good nights after dinner, when I read, picturing myself sitting with a group of intellectuals in Athens.

Actually, before I got the Symposium, around 9/11, I went through a rough few days. During the weekend of that week, I started doing my so-called pilgrimage. I walked from the hostel I stayed in, in Cebu, to the Temple of Leah in Busay. On the way back, I had also brought a copy of the Iliad with me, so I paused and read while waiting for the rain to stop. (It was a heavy rain, and it was a Collins Classics copy.) At the bottom of the mountain there was already a town, Busay town, I think. I got a haircut there, and until today I still haven’t had a new one. The walk that day was around 32,000 steps. I had a good Saturday.

During the Temple of Leah trip, I wrote to myself about this word called ‘love’. A few days after that, I bought this book and read a few pages a day after dinner (there was an authentic Thai restaurant in Cebu, thanks to a colleague!), and I finished reading it in less than two weeks. It was still an enjoyable read, and I missed that first-hand feeling of imagining I was sitting with the thinkers of Athens.

Finished at the floating cottage in Dauis, Bohol · Acquired mid-September 2026 from Fullybooked, Ayala Central Bloc, Cebu

The Republic

Plato

Finished Sep 4, 2026

Not too long after finishing the Odyssey, I picked up another Greek classic, ‘The Republic’. I had never heard of it and never knew it was a great book of Western philosophy. Probably I was already in the right mind and the right mood to dive into that theme. Reading this book was not an easy job, but compared to the Odyssey, I was able to turn the pages faster and more smoothly. The lessons I learned from this book were also not simple to comprehend, and I had to look to other sources online to ensure that I would not end up applying Plato’s or Socrates’ theories wrongly.

I enjoyed thinking about the main part of the book: just and unjust, and who each of us would be in a city, which role we would take, not by choice but by our personalities. I couldn’t help thinking along with the book about what a just city would look like if we could see it in the real world, and which country is the most accurate example of a just city. Oh, I remember I brought this book to Tubigon too, and I probably read the last chapter there, on top of a mountain in Tubigon during sunset, after a long walk from the town to the mountain.

I finished this book in Dauis at the floating cottage on an ordinary Friday morning at sunrise. I even thought of opening a café called ‘The Republic’ right here in Tagbilaran City, which I haven’t done. What this book introduced to me was not just a philosophical way of thinking, but also that I can find a path to enjoying myself with such a classic and intellectual book. I hope that isn’t too much to describe.

Finished at the floating cottage in Dauis, Bohol · Acquired July 2026 from Fullybooked, Ayala Central Bloc, Cebu

The Odyssey

Homer

Finished Aug 22, 2026

I was visiting Dumaguete for the third time, during what would be summer vacation elsewhere, though in Thailand and the Philippines it was monsoon season. It was raining even on that evening at Utopia, the first time I got to see this copy. I was in a foul mood, but I remember ordering a cup of hot hojicha latte and furiously buying this book from them. I had never known anything about the Odyssey before reading it, and it might be too shallow to say anything about it after reading it only once.

Writing today, almost three months have passed since that day. What has changed? I have accepted my mistakes. They come from my own habits and addictions, and I am not the noble man I wanted to be. Things went wrong, and I couldn’t even follow the flow. If I had been one of the soldiers in the group, I would probably have been fooled on the witch’s island and turned into a pig, and if Odysseus hadn’t saved us, I would never have made it back to my homeland to meet my wife again. If that is not too much to say...

Anyway, that's what happens in the poem, and how much of it I could bring into my real life, I couldn't say. I wish magic and fantasy were happening not only in the story but also here at my writing table in Tagbilaran. Okay, I think this is starting to get out of control. Let's pause it here.

Finished at the floating cottage in Dauis, Bohol · Acquired Aug 3, 2026 from Utopia, a secondhand bookstore and café in Dumaguete City
